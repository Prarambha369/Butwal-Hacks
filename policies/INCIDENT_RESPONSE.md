# Incident Response Guide

What happens step by step after someone reports a code of conduct violation.
Adapted from
[MLH's incident response guide](https://github.com/MLH/mlh-policies/blob/main/incident-response.md),
which is public and reusable with attribution.

## First, safety

Attendee safety comes before process. If anyone is in danger — a threat, an
assault, a medical emergency, someone who needs to leave the room — deal with
that first and report afterwards.

- If there is any general threat to attendees or staff, summon venue security
  or the police.
- If everyone is physically safe, involve security or law enforcement only at
  the affected person's request.
- Offer the affected person a private place to sit.
- Explain who you are, and ask what happened, so you can help.
- Make sure they have emergency contacts, in case they need help later.

## Who handles it

- **At an event**, the lead organizer is the first point of contact. Every
  event has one named, and the name is published before the event starts.
- **Outside an event**, the maintainer team handles it. Email
  conduct@butwalhacks.com and it reaches the people who can act.
- If you cannot reach the lead organizer, email
  legal@butwalhacks.com. It escalates to the maintainers who were not
  part of the original conversation.

You do not have to work out who is "allowed" to report. Anyone may report
anything.

## The initial report

The handler writes down what happened, as close to the report as possible. If
the reporter cannot write it themselves, the handler transcribes it as it was
told to them — in the reporter's own words.

To gather:

- Identifying details of the person the report is about.
- The behaviour that was in violation, described rather than characterised.
- The approximate time it happened.
- The circumstances around it.
- Who else was involved, and who else knows about it.

Ask about what the reporter needs first. Do not run an interview.

### What not to do

- **Do not invite the reporter to withdraw the complaint**, or mention that
  withdrawing is fine. It suggests you want them to drop it, which is
  coercive. "I will take this further if you want me to" is not coercive.
- **Do not ask the reporter how to handle the complaint**, or what penalty
  they think is fair. That is the handler's job, not the reporter's.
- **Do not investigate.** You are collecting facts, not building a case.
- **Do not promise an outcome**, and do not promise anonymity you cannot
  guarantee. You can promise that the reporter's identity will not be shared
  without their permission.

## The response

The maintainer team decides on a course of action together with the lead
organizer, and the outcome is recorded in writing with the reason.

The person the report is about is told what was reported, in a private
conversation, and given the chance to give their account. Their account is
recorded. The decision stands unless a maintainer says otherwise — but a
decision can be revisited on new information, and the reporter and the
person reported are both told if that happens.

Sanctions come from the ladder in
[DISCIPLINARY_ACTION.md](DISCIPLINARY_ACTION.md): correction, warning,
temporary suspension, permanent ban.

## Communicating about it

How an incident is handled publicly matters as much as the outcome. The rule:
everyone who knew about the incident should learn that it was not acceptable
and that action was taken — without learning anything about the private
details of what happened.

- Speak to individuals who knew about the incident but were not involved.
  Leave out the specifics.
- Announcements, if any are needed, are made by the maintainers. The local
  organizing team may be asked to deliver one, and only after the wording and
  timing have been agreed.
- Never publish the reporter's identity, the identity of the person reported,
  or the details of the incident.

If attendees are angry or distressed about how an incident was handled:

- Acknowledge the feeling, and say plainly that the experience was affected
  by the incident.
- Say that it is being taken seriously and that steps have been taken, without
  describing those steps in detail.
- Offer a clear channel for further feedback — speaking to the lead
  organizer on the day, or writing to legal@butwalhacks.com.

## Timelines

| Step | Target |
|------|--------|
| First contact with the reporter | Same day |
| Initial report written up | Within 48 hours |
| Decision on a course of action | Within 7 days |
| Decision communicated to the person reported | Within 7 days of the decision |

These are targets, not guarantees. If a case needs longer, the reporter is
told that it is taking longer and why.
