# Legal Entity and Registration

> The registration decision, what it obliges us to, and what it unblocks. Effective 16 October 2026.

## What this is

Butwal Hacks is currently an informal group with no legal personality. We intend to register as a **nonprofit business** under Nepali law. This document records the decision that is still open, compares the two structures, and lists what registration will oblige us to do.

It also records what registration **fixes** — because the absence of a legal person is the single blocker on several other obligations.

## Status: structure not yet chosen

We have not yet decided between a **non-distributing company** and an **association/NGO**. Both create a legal person. They differ in regulator, cost, renewal cycle, and what they let us do. Counsel should be asked to recommend, but the choice is ours to make.

**Until this is settled, every policy in this directory names "Butwal Hacks" without stating what it legally is.** That is a defect. A contract needs a legal person.

## The two structures

### Option A — Non-distributing company

A company incorporated for social welfare, education, science, or similar objectives, on the condition that **profits are not distributed**. Governed by section 166 of the Companies Act, registered at the **Office of the Company Registrar (OCR)** through the CAMIS portal.

| | |
|---|---|
| Regulator | Office of Company Registrar (OCR) |
| Promoters | **Minimum 5** |
| Name | **Must NOT include "Limited"** or "Pvt. Ltd." |
| Legal personality | Separate, perpetual succession |
| Liability of members | Limited |
| Conversion | **Cannot be converted into a profit-distributing company** |
| Ongoing filing | Annual return to the Company Registrar |
| Governance | Board of directors |
| Approximate cost | OCR ~NPR 15,000, plus ward office registration, rent tax, and professional fees |

### Option B — Association / NGO

Registered under the **Associations Registration Act 2034 (1977)** at the **District Administration Office (DAO)**.

| | |
|---|---|
| Regulator | District Administration Office |
| Founding members | **Minimum 7** in most districts |
| Gender requirement | **33% female representation** mandatory |
| Legal personality | Separate corporate body, perpetual succession |
| Ongoing filing | **Renewal every fiscal year** at the DAO |
| Governance | General assembly and a managing committee |
| Access to foreign funds | Requires Social Welfare Council affiliation, as does Option A |

### Comparison

| Question | Non-distributing company | Association / NGO |
|----------|------------------------|------------------|
| Can we employ staff and sign contracts? | Yes, straightforwardly | Yes, but governance is member-led |
| Can we sign subprocessor DPAs? | Yes — a company can contract | Yes |
| Annual compliance effort | Annual return | **Annual renewal**, plus returns |
| Can we ever pay dividends? | **No, permanently** | No |
| Minimum founding people | 5 | 7 |
| Fits a platform with sponsors and bounties? | **Better** | Workable, less flexible |

### Recommendation

**Option A, the non-distributing company**, for these reasons:

1. We intend to **pay bounty and prize winners**, run sponsor relationships, and hold contracts with service providers. A board-governed company with limited liability is the ordinary vehicle for that.
2. An association's annual DAO renewal is a recurring obligation for an organisation that is volunteer-run. An annual return to the Company Registrar is lighter.
3. A company is easier for international funders and commercial counterparties to transact with, and it can open a straightforward corporate bank account.
4. The constraint is permanent and one-way: a non-distributing company **cannot later become profit-distributing**. That is desirable here, and it is exactly the commitment a sponsor or donor wants to see.

Counsel must confirm the current provision. See the note on Act numbering below.

## What registration fixes

Registering does not solve everything, but it removes real blockers.

| Blocker | Effect of registering |
|---------|----------------------|
| **Cannot sign a subprocessor DPA** — the Data Protection Law requires a written transfer arrangement, and a non-distributing company cannot contract as a body | **Solved.** We can execute transfer agreements with Auth0, Supabase, Cloudinary, and the rest |
| **Directors have no legal duty** — today no individual is legally responsible for data protection | **Solved.** The duty attaches to the company and its officers, which makes the data protection role real rather than honorary |
| **Cannot employ** | Solved when we choose to hire |
| **Cannot receive foreign grants formally**, or open a corporate account, or issue a proper invoice | **Solved** |
| **Cannot get tax-exempt treatment for a defined purpose** | Solved, subject to application |

This is why priority 7 in [COMPLIANCE_GAPS.md](COMPLIANCE_GAPS.md) was ranked as blocking the cross-border work. It unblocks the largest remaining compliance task.

## What registration obliges us to do

Registration is not paperwork we file once. It creates recurring duties. This is the part most volunteer teams underestimate.

### Immediately after incorporation

| Step | Authority | Why |
|------|-----------|-----|
| Obtain **PAN** | Inland Revenue Department | Required to invoice, open a bank account, and file tax. Since Asar 15, 2083 the PAN is downloadable from CAMIS after incorporation |
| Open a **corporate bank account** | Any commercial bank | Needs the Certificate of Incorporation, PAN, MOA/AOA, and a board resolution |
| Register at the **ward office** | Local level | Required locally; attracts house rent tax and an annual business tax |
| Register with the **Social Welfare Council** | Social Welfare Council | **Mandatory for us** — see below |
| Assess **VAT** registration | Inland Revenue Department | **Requires an accountant's opinion** — see below |
| Keep statutory registers | At the registered office | Register of members, register of directors, register of charges |
| Keep books of accounts | Ongoing | Accounting standards apply |

### Recurring

| Obligation | Frequency | Authority |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| Annual return | Annual | Company Registrar (Option A) or DAO renewal (Option B) |
| Board and general assembly minutes | Ongoing | Internal, and producible on request |
| Statutory registers kept current | Ongoing | Internal |
| Accounts prepared | Annual | Accountant |
| Tax filing or exemption substantiation | Annual | Inland Revenue Department |
| Foreign contribution reporting | Annual | Social Welfare Council / concerned authority |

### When we hire

Not applicable yet — we are not employing staff. Record it now so it is not a surprise.

- **Employees Provident Fund (EPF)** under the **Employees Provident Fund Act 2019** — Karmachari Sanchaya Kosh. Applies to public and private sector employees.
- **Citizenship Improvement Trust Fund (CIT)** — separate mandatory contribution for private-sector employees.
- **Labour Act 2074 (2017)** — employment contracts, leave, working hours, record keeping, and workplace obligations.
- Registration with the Labour Office.

## Two consequences specific to Butwal Hacks

These follow directly from what the organisation intends to do, and both need professional advice.

### 1. Receiving foreign grants requires Social Welfare Council affiliation

Because Butwal Hacks intends to **receive foreign grants and work with international partners**, affiliation with the **Social Welfare Council** under the **Social Welfare Act 2049 (1992)** is mandatory. This is not optional for us, and it applies under either registration structure.

Consequences to confirm with counsel:

- What affiliate or registration category we fall into, and what the Social Welfare Council requires
- Whether we also need **INGO** status, which is a separate and higher bar, or whether we are the domestic recipient partner
- Annual reporting and renewal obligations once affiliated
- Whether our stated objectives in the MOA or constitution match the purpose of the grants, since **an exemption or affiliation can be refused if income is not applied to the stated objectives**
- Any restriction on how much of a grant we may spend on administration

### 2. Paying bounty and prize winners directly creates tax exposure

Because Butwal Hacks intends to **pay winners directly**, money will pass through the organisation rather than sponsor-to-winner. That has consequences we should not guess at.

The key question an accountant must answer:

> When a sponsor's funds arrive at Butwal Hacks and are then disbursed as a bounty award, is that income of Butwal Hacks, or a pass-through?

If it is income, then:

- **VAT registration may be required.** Thresholds are **NPR 30 lakh for services and NPR 50 lakh for goods**; sources disagree on the exact figures, so confirm the current position. Bounty disbursement and sponsorship administration are plausibly service income.
- **Income tax** may apply. The standard corporate rate is around 25%. Nonprofit status is **not** itself an income tax exemption.
- **Tax exemption, if available**, is conditional: income must be applied to the objects stated in the governing document. Spending on anything else can disqualify it.

Also confirm with Open Collective and with counsel:

- Whether Open Collective withholds or reports anything on disbursement to a Nepali entity
- Whether we must issue a tax invoice or a receipt, and whether VAT applies to that
- Whether sponsoring a minor requires the guardian account described in [PARTICIPANT_AND_GUARDIAN_TERMS.md](PARTICIPANT_AND_GUARDIAN_TERMS.md), and what that means for tax records

## A note on which Companies Act applies

Nepal's Companies Act 2081 (2024) is the current statute. **However, most published guidance on non-distributing company registration still cites Companies Act 2063 (2006) and its section 166**, including OCR-facing material and law-firm commentary we found.

We have not been able to confirm whether section 166 survives in the same form under the 2081 Act, or whether non-distributing companies moved to a different provision.

**Counsel must confirm which Act and section govern, and whether any transition provision applies to an application made now.** The practical requirements — five promoters, no "Limited" in the name, no distribution of profit, no later conversion — are consistently reported across both, so the shape of the decision is unlikely to change. The citation may still be wrong.

This document deliberately does not assert a section number as settled.

## Incorporation checklist

### Before filing

- [ ] Counsel consulted on structure choice and on the Act numbering question
- [ ] Five (Option A) or seven (Option B) founding members identified, each with citizenship certificates
- [ ] Passport photographs and signature specimens collected
- [ ] Registered office address secured, with a **rent agreement or ownership certificate**, plus the landlord's citizenship and land ownership certificate
- [ ] Name reservation sought at OCR via CAMIS — name must be unique, not misleading, and must not breach trademarks
- [ ] **MOA and AOA drafted**, stating the non-profit objects. These objects must match what we actually do, because tax exemption and Social Welfare Council affiliation both turn on them
- [ ] Board of directors or managing committee identified

### Filing

- [ ] Application filed at OCR via CAMIS (Option A) or DAO (Option B)
- [ ] Fee paid — OCR approximately NPR 15,000 for Option A
- [ ] Certificate of Incorporation obtained
- [ ] Statutory registers created: members, directors, charges

### After incorporation

- [ ] PAN obtained
- [ ] Corporate bank account opened
- [ ] Ward office registration completed
- [ ] **Social Welfare Council affiliation** — required because we receive foreign grants
- [ ] VAT position determined with an accountant
- [ ] Non-profit clause confirmed in the MOA/AOA: no distribution to members or promoters
- [ ] Legal name, registered address, and PAN inserted into every policy in this directory
- [ ] A **data protection contact appointed in writing** — now possible to attach to a real officer ([COMPLIANCE_GAPS.md](COMPLIANCE_GAPS.md), priority 4)
- [ ] Annual calendar created with filing and renewal dates
