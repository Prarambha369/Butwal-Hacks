# Community Values

The standard we hold ourselves to when we run an event. Adapted from
[MLH's community values](https://github.com/MLH/mlh-policies/blob/main/community-values.md),
which is public and reusable with attribution.

Butwal Hacks exists so that a student in Nepal can build something real,
learn from people who know things, and meet other people building things too.
These are the commitments that make that possible, and the things we hold
ourselves accountable to.

## Events set a standard for attendees

- A confirmed date and location that attendees can depend on.
- At least 24 hours of hacking, and a venue that stays open overnight for
  people who need it.
- Enough food on site to reasonably feed everyone, with options that work
  for people with dietary restrictions.
- Clear communication when anything changes — venue, schedule, theme,
  logistics — as soon as it is decided, not after people have travelled.
- Every attendee checked in on arrival, with an accurate name, email,
  phone number, and school.
- At least one lead organizer with the authority to make decisions on site at
  any hour of the event.
- Intellectual property stays with the people who made it.
- The [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) is enforced, not just
  published.

## Events are open and accessible

- No entry fee. Ever.
- Advertised early enough that people nearby can find out about it.
- No discrimination on the basis of race, religion, caste, national origin,
  colour, sex, gender identity, sexual orientation, social class, economic
  status, disability, or age.
- A public, straightforward registration process, with the criteria for
  admission published.
- Not a secret or invite-only event.
- The rules for competing are published before the event, not discovered
  during it.

## Events are fair

- Organizers do not compete in the events they organize, and neither do
  judges, mentors, or sponsors. We extend the same rule to everyone in a
  position of authority.
- Anyone may attend, whether or not they are currently a student. A recent
  graduate, a career switcher, and a first-year student are equally welcome.
- A team's eligibility is its members' eligibility. If one member does not
  meet the rules, the team does not compete.

## Events are safe and welcoming

- The [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) is adopted, published,
  linked from the event's own page, and accepted by every registrant during
  registration.
- The Code applies to everyone: hackers, volunteers, organizers, sponsors,
  judges, mentors, and the maintainers.
- There is a clear and widely known reporting system, and a named lead
  organizer to receive reports on the day.
- A first aid kit is on site.
- People who violate the Code of Conduct do not attend. An active ban is
  enforced at the door, and organizers are told who to expect.
- Nepali law is respected on drugs and alcohol, and moderation is applied
  accordingly.
- The [safeguarding policy](SAFEGUARDING_POLICY.md) is followed where
  participants under 18 are present, including guardian consent.
- Sleeping space is available where possible, and people are encouraged to
  use it.

## The values we are still working on

- **A real legal entity.** We are a volunteer community, not a registered
  organisation, and our documents do not yet carry a registration number.
- **Verified guardian consent.** Consent is a signed form, not a verified
  artefact.
- **Published follow-up.** We publish the rules in advance; we do not yet
  publish a post-event report on how an event went.
